01Overview

Porchlight: give a quick check a clear outcome.

Porchlight connects camera footage, user expectations, and events for people who still worry about home after leaving. The goal is to show evidence, explain what happened, and support a decision without requiring continuous monitoring.

02The Problem

Seeing the footage does not always answer the question.

Doors, water and electricity motivated the idea. The hackathon narrowed the demonstration to observable deliveries: stay quiet during routine activity and ask for attention when a package arrives. Lock and utility status still require appropriate devices.

Interpretation
Footage still leaves the user to decide whether activity is routine or needs action.
Repeated checking
Changing devices should not require reconstructing the same event.
Trustworthy evidence
Keep images, timestamps and connection state alongside summaries so users can check and correct them.

CEditable interpretation and trustworthy state

Plans and memory: confirmation stays with the user

Move from managing features to expressing expectations, while keeping direct editing available.

Iteration
The early home resembled a dashboard. Team feedback led to footage, one prompt, two entries and bottom input, with editing opened on demand.
Native implementation
The archived iPhone app organizes Now, Plans, Memory and Conversation. Suggestions become plans or memories only after form confirmation.

Disconnection and stale images: distinguish latest received from live

Keep recovery, timestamps and foreground/background behavior aligned with actual state.

Recovery
Retry in the foreground, reconnect after 40 seconds without a stream heartbeat, remove the live indicator after 15 seconds without an image, and pause in the background.
Image format
Phone and watch receive JPEG updates at roughly five-second intervals, not continuous audiovisual streams.

04The System

Connect the image pipeline, then validate the event loop.

A Chrome capture page uploads eufy imagery and clips. The cloud maintains shared state and provides updates over SSE/HTTPS. A shared source does not by itself validate every cross-device action.

Product and interaction
I shaped scenarios, requirements and device responsibilities, producing interaction materials that translated low interruption into concrete screens and actions.
Implementation and integration
I contributed full-stack and multi-device development and integration across capture, cloud, phone and watch. Algorithms and hardware integration were shared team responsibilities.
Pitch and communication
I structured the story from uncertainty to evidence, events and confirmation, prepared the three-device presentation, and pitched for the team.

05Outcomes

Distinguish presentation, implementation and validation.

Device pipeline
Historical integration records confirm real imagery and timestamp updates on phone and watch. The supplied recording adds routine-activity, delivery-alert and watch sequences.
Full event loop
The full loop of delivery recognition, user confirmation, cross-device consistency and archival still requires validation.
Team outcome
The team reached the Anker Hackathon finals in October 2026; this is a team result.

06Reflection & Scope

Leave the complexity to the system, and the evidence to the user.

  1. Low interruption requires information choices

    Not every change deserves an alert. Events need context and a path for user correction.

  2. Consistency does not require identical screens

    Web, phone and watch support different depths of interaction; the continuity is in the evidence and task.

Materials cover the competition demo, design records and the Oct 17, 2026 handover snapshot, not a mirror of today’s deployment. The latest watch recovery build installation, Siri launch, lock-screen push and full event loop are not claimed as completed. No user-outcome metrics are asserted.
